## Monosaccharides and stereochemistry

Monosaccharides are polyhydroxy aldehydes or ketones. Aldoses contain an aldehyde in the open-chain form; ketoses contain a ketone. Names also indicate carbon number, as in triose, pentose, and hexose.

Most monosaccharides contain several stereocenters. D and L configurations are assigned from the stereocenter farthest from the carbonyl in a Fischer projection. Epimers differ at exactly one stereocenter; enantiomers are nonsuperimposable mirror images. Enzymes distinguish these configurations with high specificity.

## Ring formation and anomers

In water, an intramolecular alcohol attacks the carbonyl to form a hemiacetal or hemiketal. Six-membered pyranoses and five-membered furanoses are common. Cyclization creates a new stereocenter at the anomeric carbon.

For a D sugar in the conventional Haworth view, the alpha anomer has its anomeric hydroxyl opposite the terminal $\mathrm{CH_2OH}$ group, while the beta anomer has it on the same side. The open-chain form permits interconversion between anomers, called mutarotation.

Chair conformations reveal steric stability better than flat Haworth formulas. Beta-D-glucopyranose is especially stable because its major substituents can occupy equatorial positions.

## Reducing sugars and glycosidic bonds

A sugar with a free anomeric carbon can open to a carbonyl form and act as a reducing sugar. When the anomeric hydroxyl forms an acetal in a glycosidic bond, that anomeric center is locked. A disaccharide remains reducing if at least one anomeric carbon is free.

Linkage notation specifies configuration and connected carbons. Maltose is Glc-$\alpha(1\rightarrow4)$-Glc, lactose is Gal-$\beta(1\rightarrow4)$-Glc, and sucrose joins both anomeric carbons, Glc-$\alpha(1\leftrightarrow2)\beta$-Fru, making it nonreducing.

## Storage polysaccharides

Starch and glycogen are glucose polymers with alpha linkages. Amylose is mainly linear with $\alpha(1\rightarrow4)$ bonds. Amylopectin adds $\alpha(1\rightarrow6)$ branches; glycogen is even more densely branched.

Branching increases solubility and creates many nonreducing ends. Enzymes can therefore add or remove glucose simultaneously at many sites, enabling rapid mobilization. Only one reducing end exists per polymer particle.

## Structural polysaccharides

Cellulose contains beta-$1\rightarrow4$-linked glucose. Its extended chains hydrogen-bond into strong fibers. Humans lack cellulase, so cellulose is dietary fiber rather than a direct glucose source. Chitin uses beta-$1\rightarrow4$-linked N-acetylglucosamine and supports arthropod exoskeletons and fungal cell walls.

The difference between starch and cellulose is not monomer identity but linkage stereochemistry. That single geometric change produces different three-dimensional structures and requires different enzymes.

## Glycoconjugates and biological information

Oligosaccharides attached to proteins and lipids create a dense, information-rich cell surface. N-linked glycans attach to asparagine, commonly within an Asn-X-Ser/Thr motif; O-linked glycans attach mainly to serine or threonine. Glycans influence folding, stability, trafficking, receptor recognition, immunity, and pathogen attachment.

Proteoglycans contain a core protein with long glycosaminoglycan chains. Repeating disaccharides often carry carboxylate and sulfate groups, producing a highly hydrated, negatively charged matrix. Hyaluronan, chondroitin sulfate, heparan sulfate, keratan sulfate, and dermatan sulfate differ in composition and modification.

These polymers resist compression, organize extracellular signals, and assemble tissues. Their biosynthesis is nontemplated: glycosyltransferases and modifying enzymes, rather than a nucleic-acid template, determine structure.

## Takeaways

- Sugar function depends on stereochemistry, ring form, and linkage position.
- A free anomeric carbon explains reducing behavior and mutarotation.
- Branching makes storage polymers rapidly accessible; beta linkages make strong structural fibers.
- Cell-surface and extracellular glycans encode recognition and mechanical properties.
