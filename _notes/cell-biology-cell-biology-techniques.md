## Preparing cells and tissues

Microscopy begins with a trade-off between structural preservation and biological activity. Fixed tissue is stabilized with cross-linking agents such as formaldehyde, embedded in wax or resin, and cut into thin sections with a microtome. Fixation preserves morphology but kills cells and may conceal epitopes. Live-cell imaging preserves dynamics but requires gentle illumination, controlled temperature and gas conditions, and labels with low toxicity.

Resolution is the ability to distinguish two nearby objects, not simply the apparent magnification. In light microscopy it is limited by diffraction to roughly half the wavelength of visible light divided by the numerical aperture. Contrast can be generated by phase contrast or differential interference contrast without staining.

## Fluorescence localization

Fluorophores absorb light at one wavelength and emit at a longer wavelength. Fluorescein emits green light after blue excitation; rhodamine emits red light after green-yellow excitation. A filter set separates excitation from emission. Antibodies localize proteins, while fluorescent in situ hybridization localizes specific DNA or RNA sequences. Polyclonal sera recognize multiple epitopes; monoclonal antibodies provide a single reproducible specificity.

Conventional wide-field fluorescence collects out-of-focus light from above and below the focal plane. Two major solutions are:

- **Deconvolution:** estimate the microscope's point-spread function and computationally reassign blurred light.
- **Confocal microscopy:** illuminate and detect a restricted focal volume, using a pinhole to reject out-of-focus emission. Serial optical sections can reconstruct a three-dimensional specimen.

## Dynamic and quantitative fluorescence

Fluorescence resonance energy transfer detects molecular proximity: an excited donor transfers energy to a compatible acceptor only across a nanometer-scale distance. It can therefore report conformational change or protein interaction in living cells.

In fluorescence recovery after photobleaching, a region is irreversibly bleached and recovery is monitored as unbleached molecules enter. The recovery rate estimates mobility; the plateau estimates the mobile fraction. Single-particle tracking gives trajectories rather than population averages.

Flow cytometry passes cells individually through a laser and measures scattering and fluorescence. Fluorescence-activated cell sorting adds charged-droplet deflection, physically isolating populations based on defined markers.

## Electron microscopy

The short effective wavelength of electrons enables much finer resolution than visible light. Transmission electron microscopy images electrons passing through a very thin specimen and reveals internal ultrastructure. Scanning electron microscopy detects electrons emitted from a scanned surface and emphasizes three-dimensional topography. Immunogold labeling couples antibodies to electron-dense gold particles, combining molecular specificity with ultrastructural localization.

Every method can introduce artifacts. A convincing conclusion therefore uses appropriate controls, independent labels, sufficient sampling, and—where possible—orthogonal methods.

## Takeaways

- Choose preparation and imaging methods according to the spatial scale and dynamics of the question.
- Confocal imaging rejects out-of-focus light; deconvolution removes it computationally.
- FRET and FRAP convert fluorescence into measurements of proximity and mobility.
- Electron microscopy provides ultrastructure, while molecular labels supply identity.
