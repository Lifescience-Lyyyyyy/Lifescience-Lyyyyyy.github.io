## Conserved mechanisms build diverse bodies

Animal forms vary, but development repeatedly uses Wnt, Hedgehog, TGF-beta, Notch, and receptor-tyrosine-kinase pathways. Evolution often changes regulatory elements and the timing or location of expression rather than inventing new proteins. Cells combine current signals with inherited transcriptional state, producing a context-dependent response and stable cell memory.

Morphogens act over a distance and generate concentration-dependent fates. Gradient shape reflects production, diffusion or transport, binding, uptake, and degradation. Cells do not merely read one concentration: they can integrate exposure over time and compare it with other pathways.

Notch-mediated lateral inhibition amplifies small differences between neighbors into alternating fates. Short-range activation combined with long-range inhibition can produce periodic patterns through reaction–diffusion. Sequential induction creates increasingly refined positional information. Asymmetric division instead segregates determinants or places daughters in different environments from the beginning.

## Axes and pattern formation

Maternal products organize the early _Drosophila_ embryo before cellularization. Bicoid and Nanos help specify anterior–posterior information, Torso controls terminal regions, and Toll signaling patterns the dorsal–ventral axis. Downstream gene networks progressively subdivide the embryo. Hox genes then assign regional identity along the body axis; their chromosomal order often parallels their expression domains.

## Morphogenesis

Pattern becomes shape through cell migration, adhesion, force, and matrix remodeling. Chemokines such as CXCL12 guide cells expressing CXCR4. Cadherin changes rearrange tissue contacts, whereas ephrin–Eph repulsion sharpens boundaries. Convergent extension narrows and lengthens a tissue by intercalation. Epithelial bending, invagination, and branching morphogenesis create tubes, vesicles, glands, lung, and kidney structures.

## Neural development

Neural progenitors acquire identities from position and birth time. Radial glia serve both as progenitors and migration scaffolds. A growth cone samples attractive and repulsive cues with its actin-rich edge, steering an axon toward intermediate and final targets. Commissural axons can switch from attraction to repulsion after crossing the midline.

EphA/ephrin-A gradients map retinal position onto the tectum. Self-avoidance molecules such as Dscam and clustered protocadherins prevent branches of one neuron from crossing while allowing neighboring neurons to overlap. Target-derived neurotrophic factors such as NGF match neuron number to target size. Synaptic activity later stabilizes useful connections and removes competitors.

## Pluripotency and reprogramming

Embryonic stem cells self-renew and can contribute to derivatives of all three germ layers. Embryoid bodies allow spontaneous three-dimensional differentiation; directed signals can generate organoids with organized tissue features. Pluripotency must be distinguished from totipotency, which includes extraembryonic lineages.

Defined transcription factors can reprogram somatic cells into induced pluripotent stem cells. Reprogramming is inefficient because it requires coordinated changes in metabolism, proliferation, cytoskeleton, enhancer use, and epigenetic state. Early changes suppress the somatic program; later changes activate the pluripotency network. Teratoma assays historically demonstrated three-germ-layer potential, while modern evaluation also uses molecular profiles and directed differentiation.

## Takeaways

- Development reuses a small signaling toolkit in different spatial and temporal combinations.
- Morphogens, lateral inhibition, and asymmetric division create patterned cell identities.
- Morphogenesis converts identity into anatomy through force, adhesion, migration, and matrix.
- Reprogramming shows that differentiated identity is actively maintained and can be reset.
