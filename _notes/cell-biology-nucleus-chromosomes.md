## Nuclear organization

The nuclear lamina, assembled from lamins and associated inner-membrane proteins, supports the envelope, anchors nuclear pores, and helps organize chromatin. Cajal bodies concentrate factors for small nuclear and nucleolar RNA maturation; nuclear speckles are enriched in pre-mRNA-splicing components. These bodies are dynamic molecular condensates rather than membrane-bound organelles.

A functional chromosome requires replication origins, a centromere for segregation, and telomeres to protect its ends. Human chromosomes contain protein-coding genes, regulatory elements, repetitive DNA, transposable-element remnants, and many noncoding RNAs.

## Nucleosomes and chromatin remodeling

About 147 base pairs of DNA wrap 1.7 turns around a histone octamer. Positively charged lysine and arginine residues help neutralize DNA, but nucleosome formation also requires strong bending of the double helix. Linker histone and interactions between neighboring nucleosomes promote compaction.

Nucleosomes are not fixed barriers. ATP-dependent remodeling complexes slide, eject, or restructure them, and histone chaperones exchange variants. The centromeric histone variant CENP-A helps specify centromere identity; therefore centromeres are defined epigenetically as well as by repetitive sequence.

## Covalent modification and epigenetic memory

DNA methyltransferases add methyl groups, commonly at CpG dinucleotides. Maintenance enzymes copy patterns after replication, whereas developmental reprogramming erases and re-establishes much of the methylome. Genomic imprinting preserves parent-of-origin-specific expression at selected loci.

Histone tails can be acetylated, methylated, phosphorylated, or ubiquitylated. Acetylation often weakens chromatin contacts, but the most general principle is recruitment: reader proteins recognize a mark and bring remodeling, transcriptional, or silencing machinery. Reader–writer coupling can spread a chromatin state and make it self-propagating. Boundaries and active processes prevent unlimited spreading.

## Three-dimensional genome organization

Chromosomes occupy territories and form loops and contact domains. Cohesin-driven loop extrusion and boundary factors help organize many mammalian domains. Chromosome-conformation-capture methods cross-link nearby DNA, ligate interacting fragments, and quantify contacts. Population maps reveal average proximity, not a single rigid structure, and individual cells remain dynamic.

## Genome evolution

Mutation, duplication, recombination, and mobile elements continually reshape genomes. LINEs, SINEs, retroviral-like elements, and DNA transposons constitute a large fraction of many genomes. Gene duplication can produce redundancy, altered dosage, pseudogenes, or new functions. Comparative genomics maps conservation onto phylogenetic trees: unusually slow change suggests purifying selection, while lineage-specific acceleration can indicate altered function or regulation.

## Takeaways

- Chromatin packages DNA while preserving regulated access.
- Remodeling, histone variants, and covalent marks create specialized chromosomal states.
- Epigenetic inheritance is stable enough for memory but reversible during development.
- The genome is a dynamic three-dimensional and evolutionary system, not a linear text alone.
