Biochemistry / Chapter 19

Seven major carbon-skeleton products

After nitrogen removal, amino-acid carbons enter metabolism as pyruvate, acetyl-CoA, acetoacetate, alpha-ketoglutarate, succinyl-CoA, fumarate, or oxaloacetate.

Glucogenic amino acids yield pyruvate or citric-acid-cycle intermediates that can support net glucose synthesis. Ketogenic amino acids yield acetyl-CoA or acetoacetate and cannot produce net glucose in animals because acetyl-CoA carbon is lost as carbon dioxide and the pyruvate dehydrogenase reaction is irreversible. Leucine and lysine are exclusively ketogenic; several other amino acids are both glucogenic and ketogenic.

Entry families

  • Alanine, serine, glycine, cysteine, and part of threonine feed pyruvate.
  • Glutamate, glutamine, proline, arginine, and histidine feed alpha-ketoglutarate through glutamate.
  • Aspartate and asparagine feed oxaloacetate.
  • Valine, isoleucine, methionine, and threonine can form propionyl-CoA and then succinyl-CoA.
  • Phenylalanine and tyrosine yield fumarate plus acetoacetate.
  • Branched-chain amino acids begin oxidation largely outside liver and use a dehydrogenase complex related to PDH.

Organizing pathways by entry product is more useful than memorizing isolated reaction lists because it predicts both energetic fate and biosynthetic connections.

One-carbon carriers

Tetrahydrofolate carries one-carbon units at several oxidation states on N5 and N10. Serine hydroxymethyltransferase is a major source of 5,10-methylene-THF, converting serine to glycine. Folate-derived units support purine synthesis, thymidylate synthesis, and remethylation of homocysteine.

Methionine is activated with ATP to form S-adenosylmethionine, a major methyl donor. After methyl transfer, S-adenosylhomocysteine becomes homocysteine. Homocysteine can be remethylated to methionine using folate and vitamin B12 or directed through the transsulfuration pathway to cysteine using vitamin B6.

Biotin carries activated carbon dioxide, while vitamin B12 supports difficult rearrangements and methyl transfer. Deficiency can therefore trap folate in a metabolically unavailable form and impair both blood-cell formation and neural function.

Selected pathways and disorders

Phenylalanine hydroxylase converts phenylalanine to tyrosine using tetrahydrobiopterin. Deficiency causes phenylketonuria; treatment restricts phenylalanine while ensuring adequate tyrosine. Tyrosine also supplies catecholamines, thyroid hormone, and melanin. Defects in downstream tyrosine degradation cause disorders such as alkaptonuria, while defects in melanin synthesis can cause albinism.

Branched-chain alpha-keto acid dehydrogenase deficiency causes maple syrup urine disease. Accumulating branched-chain amino acids and keto acids are neurotoxic. Methylmalonyl-CoA mutase or vitamin B12 defects cause methylmalonic acidemia.

Tryptophan supplies serotonin, melatonin, and nicotinamide-related metabolites as well as degradative carbon. Histidine degradation transfers a formimino group to tetrahydrofolate; folate deficiency can cause accumulation of the donor intermediate.

Metabolic interpretation

Inherited defects often become severe during fasting, illness, or high protein intake because catabolic flux rises. Clinical phenotypes reflect not only loss of product but also accumulation of toxic substrates, diversion into side pathways, cofactor limitation, and tissue-specific transport.

Illustrated reference

Takeaways

  • Amino-acid carbon converges on seven central metabolic products.
  • Glucogenic and ketogenic labels follow from those entry points.
  • THF, SAM, biotin, PLP, and vitamin B12 solve distinct one-carbon or rearrangement problems.
  • Metabolic disease often combines product deficiency with toxic intermediate accumulation.
