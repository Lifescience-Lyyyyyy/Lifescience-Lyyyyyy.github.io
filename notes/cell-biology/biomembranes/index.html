Cell Biology / Chapter 5

The bilayer as a self-sealing fluid

Cell membranes are roughly 5 nm thick. Their principal lipids—glycerophospholipids, sphingolipids, and sterols—are amphipathic. The hydrophobic effect drives tails away from water while polar heads remain hydrated, producing a bilayer that closes into a sealed compartment. Lipids diffuse rapidly within one leaflet, whereas spontaneous movement between leaflets is slow.

Membrane fluidity depends on temperature and composition. Shorter chains and cis double bonds weaken packing and increase fluidity. Cholesterol buffers the response: it restrains phospholipid movement at high temperature but prevents tight packing at low temperature. Cells adjust lipid synthesis to preserve a useful physical state.

Asymmetry and membrane domains

The two leaflets have different compositions. New phospholipids are synthesized mainly on the cytosolic face of the endoplasmic reticulum and equilibrated by scramblases. Flippases and floppases later create selective asymmetry. Phosphatidylserine is normally cytosolic; its exposure outside is a signal during apoptosis. Glycolipids face the noncytosolic surface, which becomes the extracellular face after vesicle fusion.

Membranes can contain dynamic nanoscale domains enriched in cholesterol, sphingolipids, and selected proteins. These are not rigid islands: their size and lifetime depend on protein interactions, cortical actin, and trafficking. Lipid droplets are distinct organelles bounded by a phospholipid monolayer because their neutral-lipid core buds from between the two ER leaflets.

Membrane proteins

Alpha helices are the most common transmembrane segments; a hydrophobic stretch shields the peptide backbone and contacts lipid tails. Beta barrels form larger pores in bacterial, mitochondrial, and chloroplast outer membranes. Other proteins attach through amphipathic helices, lipid anchors, peripheral interactions, or glycosylphosphatidylinositol anchors.

Detergents solubilize membrane proteins by surrounding their hydrophobic surfaces. At the critical micelle concentration, detergent molecules form micelles; too little leaves protein insoluble, while harsh detergent can disrupt structure. Purified proteins can be reconstituted into liposomes or nanodiscs to test sufficiency and transport function.

Confinement, mechanics, and curvature

Lateral diffusion is fast but not unlimited. Tight junctions form fences between apical and basolateral domains. Oligomerization, extracellular contacts, and cytoskeletal corrals restrict movement. The cortical cytoskeleton distributes force and prevents the plasma membrane from behaving like an unsupported film.

Membranes bend when one leaflet expands relative to the other, when amphipathic helices insert shallowly, or when curved protein scaffolds assemble. These mechanisms cooperate in budding, tubulation, endocytosis, and organelle shaping.

Takeaways

  • Amphipathic lipids spontaneously form a fluid, self-sealing bilayer.
  • Lipid composition controls thickness, fluidity, curvature, and protein recruitment.
  • Leaflet asymmetry is actively generated and carries biological information.
  • Membrane domains arise from lipids, proteins, trafficking, and the cytoskeleton together.
