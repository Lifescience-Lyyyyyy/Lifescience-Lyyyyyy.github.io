Cell Biology / Chapter 15

A junctional map of epithelia

Animal tissues connect cells to one another and to extracellular matrix. Tight junctions seal the paracellular space. Adherens junctions connect cadherins to actin. Desmosomes connect cadherins to intermediate filaments. Gap junctions create intercellular channels. Focal adhesions connect integrins to actin, whereas hemidesmosomes connect integrins to intermediate filaments and basal lamina.

Cadherins and tissue organization

Classical cadherins require extracellular Ca2+, which stiffens their repeated domains. They usually bind homophilically: E-cadherin-rich cells adhere preferentially to similar cells. Many individually weak interactions form a strong but reversible contact. Different cadherin expression and cortical tension help sort cells during morphogenesis; loss of E-cadherin is a major feature of epithelial-to-mesenchymal transition.

P120-, beta-, and alpha-catenin connect classical cadherins to actin regulators. Tension can expose binding sites and reinforce the junction, making adhesion mechanosensitive. Coordinated contraction and junction remodeling drive cell intercalation and tissue elongation. Desmogleins and desmocollins instead form desmosomes that distribute stress through intermediate-filament networks.

Barriers and communication

Claudin-family proteins form selective tight-junction strands, while occludin and junctional adhesion molecules contribute regulation. ZO scaffold proteins link these strands to actin. Tight junctions both seal between cells and fence apical from basolateral membrane proteins.

A gap-junction channel forms when two connexons dock; each connexon contains six connexins. Ions and small metabolites pass directly, supporting electrical and metabolic coupling. Plants use plasmodesmata, membrane-lined channels that traverse cell walls and contain an ER-derived desmotubule.

Extracellular matrix

Glycosaminoglycans are negatively charged polysaccharides that attract water and form compressible gels. Except for hyaluronan, they are covalently attached to core proteins as proteoglycans. Hyaluronan is synthesized directly at the plasma membrane and serves as a space-filling and migratory matrix.

Collagen resists tension; type I dominates skin and bone, while type IV forms sheet-like basal-lamina networks. Elastin gives arteries, lung, and skin recoil. Fibronectin and laminins are multidomain glycoproteins that organize matrix and bind receptors. The basal lamina contains laminin, type IV collagen, nidogen, and perlecan and serves structural, filtering, polarity, and regenerative roles.

Integrins and mechanotransduction

Integrins are alpha/beta heterodimers that switch between bent low-affinity and extended high-affinity states. Intracellular signals activate them from inside out; matrix binding then signals outside in. Clustering creates avidity and recruits talin, vinculin, focal-adhesion kinase, and actin. Stretching talin exposes vinculin sites, converting force into molecular assembly. Integrin signaling controls migration, proliferation, survival, and anchorage dependence.

Plant walls solve a related mechanical problem with cellulose microfibrils embedded in pectins and other polysaccharides. The orientation of cellulose deposition constrains the direction of cell expansion and therefore tissue shape.

Takeaways

  • Junctions connect specific cytoskeletal systems to neighboring cells or matrix.
  • Adhesion molecules organize tissues and also transmit biochemical and mechanical signals.
  • The extracellular matrix is an active, cell-built signaling environment.
  • Integrins couple matrix mechanics to the cytoskeleton and cell fate.
