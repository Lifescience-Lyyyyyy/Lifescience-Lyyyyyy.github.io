Cell Biology / Chapter 9

The trafficking map

The biosynthetic–secretory route moves cargo from ER to Golgi and then to the plasma membrane, secretory vesicles, or endolysosomal system. Endocytosis moves membrane and extracellular material inward. Retrieval pathways return escaped resident proteins and recycle transport machinery. Membrane topology is conserved: a luminal domain remains noncytosolic throughout traffic.

Budding, targeting, and fusion

Coats select cargo and bend membranes. COPII carries material from ER toward Golgi; COPI supports Golgi-to-ER retrieval and intra-Golgi traffic; clathrin functions at the plasma membrane and trans-Golgi network. Small GTPases initiate coat assembly. Sar1-GTP inserts into ER membrane and recruits the inner COPII coat Sec23/Sec24, followed by the Sec13/Sec31 outer cage. GTP hydrolysis helps uncoat the carrier.

Phosphoinositides mark membrane identity and recruit adaptors. Rab-GTPases and their effectors capture vesicles at the proper destination. Pairing of vesicle and target SNAREs then forms a tight trans-SNARE bundle that forces the bilayers together. NSF and SNAP proteins use ATP to disassemble the post-fusion complex. These layers of recognition make fusion selective without relying on one molecule alone.

ER–Golgi traffic and glycan processing

COPII exit sites preferentially package properly folded cargo. Soluble ER residents bearing a C-terminal KDEL-like signal bind retrieval receptors in the Golgi and return through COPI traffic. The Golgi is ordered from cis entry cisternae to the trans-Golgi network. Progressive glycosidases and glycosyltransferases remodel N-linked glycans and add O-linked sugars and glycosaminoglycans.

Glycosylation assists folding, protects proteins from proteolysis, increases solubility, and creates recognition sites. Lysosomal hydrolases acquire mannose 6-phosphate, bind receptors in the trans-Golgi network, and are released as endosomes acidify. Loss of the tagging enzyme misroutes many hydrolases and causes inclusion-cell disease.

Lysosomes and autophagy

Late endosomes receive endocytic cargo and lysosomal enzymes, mature into endolysosomes, and eventually regenerate lysosomes. Acid hydrolases degrade proteins, lipids, nucleic acids, and polysaccharides. Plant and fungal vacuoles add storage, ion balance, and turgor control.

Macroautophagy encloses cytoplasm in a double-membrane autophagosome that fuses with lysosomes. Starvation induces bulk recycling; selective autophagy removes damaged mitochondria, peroxisomes, protein aggregates, or invading microbes. Blocking vacuolar degradation allowed Yoshinori Ohsumi to visualize accumulated autophagic bodies and genetically dissect the pathway.

Endocytosis and exocytosis

Pinocytosis continually samples fluid. Receptor-mediated endocytosis concentrates selected cargo such as LDL in clathrin-coated pits. Early endosomes sort receptors for recycling, degradation, or retrograde transport. ESCRT complexes package ubiquitylated membrane proteins into intraluminal vesicles. Caveolae and macropinocytosis provide alternative uptake routes; phagocytes engulf large particles with actin-driven pseudopods.

Constitutive exocytosis continuously delivers membrane and secreted products. Regulated secretory granules mature near the surface and fuse only after a signal. At synapses, primed vesicles respond within milliseconds to local Ca2+, and compensatory endocytosis retrieves vesicle membrane.

Illustrated reference

Takeaways

  • Coats shape carriers and select cargo; Rabs tether; SNAREs execute fusion.
  • The Golgi modifies and sorts cargo while retrieval preserves compartment identity.
  • Lysosomes receive material from endocytosis, phagocytosis, and autophagy.
  • Recycling is as important as forward flow for maintaining organelles and the plasma membrane.
